Writing a deck

A deck is an ordinary markdown file — no separators, no frontmatter. Every heading becomes a card, and a heading branches off the nearest shallower heading above it.

# Product            ← root card
Intro text.

## Pricing           ← child of Product
### Enterprise       ← child of Pricing
## Roadmap {#plan}   ← child of Product, id "plan"

See the [roadmap](#plan).

Rules

  • Text before the first heading becomes a root card of its own.
  • Headings inside fenced code blocks are ignored.
  • Card ids are the heading text slugified (## Moving around → moving-around), or set one with {#id} at the end of the heading.
  • A link to #id flies to that card. The address bar keeps the current card, so links to a card can be shared.
  • Older decks that separate slides with +++ and use id / parent / x / y frontmatter still load.

reveal.js decks

Decks written for reveal.js open as they are. --- starts a new slide and -- a vertical one beneath it; the first slide becomes the root card. Supported: fragments (fade-up, fade-in-then-out, grow, …, revealed one per →), r-stack, <!-- .element: … -->, <section> slides, data-autoslide (while presenting), Graphviz ```dot diagrams, ECharts echarts-container charts, data-gsap entrance animations and title-scramble. Cards widen for wide tables, columns, diagrams and embeds, and anything wider still is scaled to fit.

Images and loading from the web

Open a deck with slidetake/?src=https://…/deck.md and its relative images load from next to it. For a file loaded from disk, add base: https://…/deck-folder/ to the top block. ?edit=0 hides the editor and ?layout=tree|radial|path|focus picks the layout.

Layouts

tree is a top-down chart and radial rings out from the root. For video, path is a staircase in the order you present them: each section is a row (1 → 1.1 → 1.2), and the next section starts just below where it ended (↓ 2 → 2.1 → 2.2), so every move is a short step right or down, and focus doesn't travel at all: the next slide fades in. Both show a progress bar while presenting that moves with every press.

Text keeps one size within a section; it only changes when the section does. <!-- view: overview --> on a slide pulls back to the whole map for a moment before flying in, a good beat at the start of a chapter.

Moving around

Click a card. → ← / Space walk in document order, ↓ ↑ child / parent, O overview, 1–4 switch layout, E editor, F fullscreen, R record and C camera (with ?obs), L light / dark (or mode: light in the deck, ?mode=light in the address).

Drag to pan. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

Recording mode: ?obs

The camera, recording and talk timer are for making a video, so they only appear when the address says where OBS is: ?obs=localhost:4455 or ?obs=host:port:password (a bare ?obs uses the connection you remembered). Without it you get the deck and Fullscreen, and camera settings in the deck are ignored, so the same deck works for readers on a website.

Channel mark and PIP camera

An optional block at the very top of the file sets deck-wide options:

---
brand: sakthipriyan.com/building-wealth   ← text in the top-right corner
brand_style: text            ← text (default) or chip
logo: https://…/logo.png     ← image beside it (URL or data: URI)
pip: bottom-right            ← top-left | top-right | bottom-left | bottom-right | none
pip_size: 24%                ← width of the camera box
pip_shape: rect              ← rect (16:9) or circle
pip_mirror: yes              ← mirror the webcam like a selfie view
camera: 24%, 40%, full, off  ← layouts V cycles through
---

Slides are always placed clear of the brand and the PIP box. With ◉ Camera on, your webcam shows live inside the box (it starts when you switch it on, press P, or reach a camera step), so a plain screen recording captures you and the slides together. Without the webcam, the box is a dashed guide for lining up an OBS camera overlay.

Camera per slide

Put a comment anywhere in a slide to change the camera from that slide onward (other markdown viewers hide it):

## Intro
<!-- camera: full -->        ← camera fills the screen

## Pricing
<!-- camera: 18% flip -->    ← small, on the other side

## Q&A
<!-- camera: off -->

List several layouts with commas to step through them on one slide: <!-- camera: full, on --> opens on you full-screen, the next → shrinks the camera into its corner and reveals the slide, and the one after moves on. ← steps back the same way.

Once the camera is on, P starts the webcam for decks that set camera: or have camera comments.

Words you can combine: on off full a size like 30%, a corner, flip (other side), circle / rect, mirror / unmirror. A slide always gets the nearest setting at or before it, so going back shows the same camera as going forward.

V steps through the camera: list for the current slide (default: on → full → off); the next slide goes back to its own setting. C or the Camera button switches the camera off for the whole deck: camera comments are ignored, steps that only change the camera are skipped, and slides use the whole screen. The camera is off every time the page opens; turn it on when you want it.

Progress and talk timer

While presenting, a thin bar along the bottom fills in the theme colour with every press. Give the deck a length, duration: 10m (or 90s, 1h, 12:30, or ?duration=10m in the address), and a half-strength layer of the same colour grows behind it with the clock: if that lighter stretch shows past the solid fill, you're behind; if it reaches the end, the bar pulses. The timer is for you, so recorded takes show progress only unless the deck says timer: always. progress: off hides the bar.

Recording a take

● Record connects to OBS over its WebSocket server, counts down, starts the recording, and from then on the deck drives everything: camera layouts, reveals and slides follow → (a Bluetooth clicker works). Clicks, dragging and the map are locked so the take only moves forward. ← takes the current slide again, keeping every attempt; B pauses; one press past the last step stops OBS.

Then the review opens: every slide with its takes (the last one picked), previews straight from the OBS file, a ready-to-run ffmpeg command for the picked takes, and the YouTube title, description with chapters and links, and tags. Sessions are kept in this browser, so a take can be reviewed again later from ● Record. ?obs=host:port:password in the address fills in OBS and connects automatically. Deck front matter summary, related (a list of [Title](url)), tags / wealth_tags / systems_tags, and permalink or site feed the publish pack.

Recording

The current slide is scaled up to fill the screen. + − make slides bigger or smaller, and the size is remembered. P goes fullscreen; the cursor and controls hide after two seconds without mouse movement.

Your draft is kept in this browser only. Use Download to keep a file.

Record a take

OBS

Not connected

In OBS: Tools → WebSocket Server Settings → enable it; the port and password are shown there. Point OBS at this browser window plus your microphone. No scenes are needed: the deck switches the camera itself.

During the take

  • → / clicker next: the next step. One more press after the last step ends the take and stops OBS.
  • ← / clicker back: take this slide again. Every attempt is kept; you pick the best one afterwards.
  • B / clicker blank: pause and resume the recording.
  • Esc: stop the take early.

Review the take

Pick the file OBS saved (.mov, .mp4 or .mkv) to preview takes. Or choose your OBS recordings folder once, and later takes load by themselves.
